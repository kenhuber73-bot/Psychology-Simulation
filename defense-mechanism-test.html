What's Your Defense Style?

A self-assessment exploring the psychological defense mechanisms we all use to cope with pain we'd rather not feel.

Before you begin

Psychological Defense Mechanisms

Every one of us pushes away feelings, needs, or truths that feel too painful to face head-on — that's not a flaw, it's a normal part of being human. Psychologists call these coping strategies defense mechanisms. They operate mostly outside our awareness, which is exactly what makes them hard to spot in ourselves.

This activity walks you through four steps:

1
Take the test. Rate 52 short statements honestly, based on how you actually tend to think, feel, and act — not how you wish you were.
2
See your top 3. The assessment identifies the three defense mechanisms you lean on most, out of 13 possible.
3
Understand where it comes from. Each result explains what the mechanism does and the kinds of experiences that tend to produce it.
4
Reflect and grow. You'll get reflection activities for each of your top 3 defenses, with space to write your own responses.
This is not a validated psychological test and cannot diagnose anything. It's an educational self-reflection tool for a psychology class, meant to help you notice patterns in how you cope, not to label you. Be honest rather than trying to look a certain way — no one else sees your individual answers unless you choose to share them, and nothing is saved to a server.
