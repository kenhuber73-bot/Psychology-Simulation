This is based on Loftus & Palmer's classic 1974 study. Participants who watched the same footage but were asked with a more intense verb (like "smashed") gave higher speed estimates than those asked with a milder verb (like "contacted") — and in a follow-up, "smashed" participants were also more likely to falsely remember seeing broken glass that was never in the film. Memory isn't a recording; it's reconstructed each time you recall it, and the wording of a question can quietly reshape what you "remember." Your result here is just one data point, not a diagnosis of your memory.
