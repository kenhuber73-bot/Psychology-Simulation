This is negative reinforcement

Negative reinforcement means a behavior gets strengthened because it makes something unpleasant go away. The word "negative" here doesn't mean "bad" — it means something was subtracted (the alarm), not added. You weren't told the rule (click the buttons right-to-left) — you had to discover it by acting and noticing what worked, the same way Thorndike's cats learned to escape puzzle boxes by trial and error. Every time you found the right sequence, the alarm stopped, and that relief is exactly what reinforcement is: a consequence that makes the behavior that produced it more likely — and, as your chart probably shows, faster and less error-prone — the next time.

Don't confuse it with these

Positive reinforcementAdd something pleasant to increase a behavior. (Lucky Orchard's gems)
Negative reinforcementRemove something unpleasant to increase a behavior. (This activity)
PunishmentAdd or remove something to decrease a behavior — the opposite goal entirely.

You do this every day

Hitting snooze to stop an alarm, buckling your seatbelt to stop the dinging, putting on a jacket to stop feeling cold, taking a painkiller to stop a headache, closing a tab to stop a notification sound — every one of these is negative reinforcement. The behavior isn't rewarded with something new; it's reinforced because it makes something bad stop.
